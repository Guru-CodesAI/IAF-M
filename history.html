Prelude to Conflict

DECEMBER 1971

The prelude to the 1971 war was marked by escalating tensions on the eastern border. The Indian Air Force (IAF), operating under a mandate of rapid response and overwhelming force, meticulously planned a campaign to neutralize enemy air capabilities and support ground offensives.

This timeline reconstructs the pivotal air operations, drawing from declassified logs and official military histories, presenting the sheer scale of the aerial theater.

"The outcome of the ground war was heavily dictated by the swift establishment of air superiority in the opening hours of the conflict."

— Official History of the 1971 Indo-Pak War

CHRONOLOGICAL RECORD

Key Operations Timeline

SOURCE: OFFICIAL HISTORY OF THE 1971 WAR (MoD) / PIB DECLASSIFIED ARCHIVES

3 DEC 1971

Day 1 — Western Front

Operation Cactus Lily — Pre-emptive Strikes

Pakistan Air Force launches pre-emptive strikes on Indian airfields at Amritsar, Pathankot, Srinagar, Awantipur, Uttarlai, Jodhpur, Ambala, and Agra. The IAF responds within hours, launching counter-air operations across the western sector. IAF Gnats and Hunters scramble to intercept PAF Sabres and Starfighters.

WESTERN SECTOR COUNTER-AIR OPS

4 DEC 1971

Day 2 — Eastern Front

Air Superiority Established Over East Pakistan

IAF achieves near-total air superiority over East Pakistan within 48 hours. Hunters, Gnats, and Su-7s systematically destroy PAF's Eastern Air Command assets at Tejgaon, Kurmitola, Tezgaon, and Jessore airfields. The PAF's 14 Squadron — the sole fighter unit in the east — is effectively neutralized, leaving ground forces without air cover.

EASTERN SECTOR AIR SUPERIORITY

5–8 DEC 1971

Days 3–6 — Naval Strike

Operation Trident & Python — Karachi Harbour Strikes

In a landmark joint operation, IAF Alizé maritime patrol aircraft coordinate with the Indian Navy's missile boats to strike Karachi harbour. IAF Canberras conduct night bombing raids on the Karachi oil storage facilities, igniting fires visible for days. The strikes cripple Pakistan's primary naval logistics hub and fuel supply chain, marking the first use of anti-ship missiles in the subcontinent.

MARITIME OPS JOINT OPERATION

9–14 DEC 1971

Days 7–12 — Close Air Support

Close Air Support — Advance on Dhaka

With air superiority secured, IAF assets pivot to close air support for the rapid Indian Army advance toward Dhaka. Su-7 Fitters and Hunters fly hundreds of sorties, destroying fortified bunkers, bridges, and troop concentrations. The psychological impact of unchallenged IAF operations accelerates the collapse of Pakistani resistance. Paratroopers are airlifted by IAF An-12s to seize key positions ahead of the ground advance.

CLOSE AIR SUPPORT STRATEGIC AIRLIFT

16 DEC 1971

Day 14 — Victory

Instrument of Surrender — Liberation of Bangladesh

Pakistani Lt. Gen. A.A.K. Niazi signs the Instrument of Surrender at Dhaka's Ramna Race Course, surrendering 93,000 troops — the largest military capitulation since World War II. The IAF flew over 4,000 sorties in 14 days, losing 45 aircraft while destroying 94 PAF aircraft. The campaign is studied globally as a model of rapid air-land integration and decisive force application.
